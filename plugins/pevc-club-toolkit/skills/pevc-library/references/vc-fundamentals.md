# VC fundamentals

Module 1 of the club library. Sources: *Intro to VC* (Avidan Rudansky), *Venture Capital 101* (Brian Rothenberg), *Basics of Financing* (Jeff Bussgang, Flybridge/HBS, Jan 2021), *How Venture Capital Works* (Bob Zider, HBR), *Understanding how VCs think* (Guhesh Ramanathan), *The Ultimate VC Metrics Cheat Sheet* (Diligent Equity).

## What venture capital is

A form of private equity that makes long-term, minority equity investments in private companies that cannot raise traditional financing. Returns come only from a liquidity event — acquisition or IPO — typically 5–10 years out. High risk, and the return has to justify it.

Zider's point, still the useful corrective: VC funds very little basic innovation. More than 80% of venture money goes into *building the infrastructure to grow* — manufacturing, marketing, sales, working capital — not into invention. It fills a gap left by the rules of other capital markets: banks need hard assets to lend against, and public markets historically needed real revenue and a profit history.

## Who is who

- **LPs (limited partners)** — pension funds, endowments, family offices. Commit 98%+ of the capital.
- **GPs (general partners)** — run the fund, make the decisions, sit on boards. Commit 1–2% of fund size themselves so incentives line up.
- **Principals / associates / analysts** — associates source deals, run diligence and write the investment memos; analysts do similar work more junior. Principals help portfolio companies but do not decide.
- **Venture partners and EIRs** — part-time, bring deal flow and judgement.

The fund is several legal entities: a management company (pays salaries from the fee), a GP entity (receives carry), and the fund itself (where LP money sits).

## The economics: "2 and 20"

- **~2% management fee** per year on committed capital, covering all operating costs. It usually steps down after the commitment period.
- **20% carried interest** on profits, and only after LPs get their capital back and the hurdle is cleared — commonly quoted at 7–8% IRR, with one library source using ~12% a year as the LP's demanded return.

Consequence worth stating: a $100m fund paying 2% for ten years spends ~$20m on fees, leaving ~$80m to invest. There is structural pressure to raise bigger funds, because the fee scales with size.

## Lifecycle

- **Commitment / investment period** — about 5 years, when new companies are added.
- **Fund life** — about 10 years, extensions limited.
- Reserves are allocated up front for follow-on rounds. This is why firms raise a new fund every 3–5 years.

## The power law

Returns concentrate. Of 45–50 investments in a fund, typically only 1–2 return the entire fund and 6–8 produce a fund-level return. One library worked example: ten $8m investments at 25% ownership, needing roughly $310m back — ten $50m exits give $125m; five at $50m plus five at $100m give $187.5m; only a very large outlier clears the bar.

This is why "could be a good business" and "is a venture investment" are different judgements.

## Stage ladder (US patterns, Flybridge 2021 — check current market before quoting)

| Stage | Raise | Post-money | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | $250–500k | $5–7m cap | SAFE / convertible note |
| Seed | $1–3m | $8–15m | Note, SAFE or priced |
| Series A | $5–10m | $20–50m | Priced, board seat, control terms |
| Series B | $20–40m | $80–200m | Priced, control terms, seniority |

## VCs vs angels

| | VC | Angel |
|---|---|---|
| Control | Wants voting rights, board, vetoes | Usually wants none |
| Ownership target | 10–20% | 1–10% |
| Engagement | Actively engaged; it is their job | Varies — often a hobby or a personal mission |
| Failure mode | Inexperience can do real harm | Can be naïve, irrational, emotional |

## When VC is the wrong instrument

Bussgang's two warnings, worth repeating to any founder-side student:

1. **VC shortens runway.** Venture money funds a higher burn rate. That is a good trade when the model works and a bad one while you are still searching for it.
2. **VC narrows exit options.** The statistically most likely startup exit is an acquisition under $50m. That outcome does little for a fund, so incentives diverge.

Also from the library: investors see 300–500 deals for every one they fund, and most form a view in the first 10–15 minutes. Pattern recognition cuts both ways — the same research notes documented bias in how founders are questioned.

## The 5 Ts (Ramanathan's shorthand for what VCs screen on)

**Team** — cohesive and able to execute · **TAM** — genuinely large · **Technology** — leverageable and scalable · **Traction** — growing now · **Trenches** — the defensive position.

## Fund metrics in one line each

- **MOIC** — multiple on invested capital. Raw picking skill.
- **Gross / Net TVPI** — total value (realized + paper) ÷ paid-in capital. Unqualified "TVPI" normally means net.
- **RVPI** — the unrealized half. Flattering early; only matters if it becomes DPI.
- **DPI** — cash distributed ÷ paid-in. The metric that settles the argument at the end of a fund.
- **IRR** — gross vs net differ materially; always say which, and whether the base is paid-in or invested capital.
